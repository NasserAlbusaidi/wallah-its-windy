    What this model knows

    Observed inputs

    OISST sea-surface temperatures, ERA5 steering winds and the full 200–850 hPa shear vector, IBTrACS tracks, and GMRT terrain shape the sandbox.

    Deliberate simplifications

    Track and intensity remain a deterministic point-storm model. ERA5 mid-level humidity, WOA23 upper-ocean heat content, persistent core organization, and a storm-generated cold wake now couple into intensity. A parametric Holland wind field derives pressure, eyewall scale, outer-core size, and wind radii; none are aircraft observations. Wadi light follows timed HydroSHEDS D8 routes, but it is not a discharge or inundation model.

    Physical structure

    The amber ring marks radius of maximum wind. Cyan contours show the parametric 34- and 64-knot footprints. Forward motion strengthens the right side; deep-layer shear stretches the outer wind field and shifts separate eyewall and spiral-rainband rain downshear-left; terrain adds an upslope rainfall component. The RMW climatology is an Atlantic-derived proxy, explicitly bounded for this North Indian Ocean sandbox.

    Historic environments

    Ten observed hindcasts start from the first tropical-storm fix safely inside the map, simulate freely with no track nudging, and score the result against the faint observed track. Seven storms calibrate the bounded intensity search; three complete storms remain an untouched holdout. Counterfactual mode instead places your storm into the same event environment. Neither mode is an operational forecast.

    Product mode and valid time

    The permanent identity bar separates the climatology sandbox, historical hindcasts, and historical counterfactuals. Climatology has model elapsed time but no UTC valid time. Event runs derive UTC model time from their frozen event or hindcast origin. Synthetic storms use neutral SIM identifiers, never official cyclone names. Observed imagery is display-only and requires confirmation on an active run; the bar marks it time-matched, pending, or unsynced against the model clock.

    Weather views

    SST, humidity, ocean heat, and shear show the exact baked field being sampled by the model. The wind view superposes the active ERA5 steering field with the parametric Holland vortex. The generated cloud field adds restrained context to wind, terrain, rain-radar, and storm-total rainfall while leaving the environmental diagnostics clear. On infrared, the satellite desk offers enhanced IR, grayscale, and daytime visible-style palettes. It can show the simulated cloud field, timestamp-labelled Meteosat/INSAT pixels when available, or a six-hour visual handoff from observation to simulation. Observed pixels never change the model state; this is not data assimilation. The radar desk separately offers a recent, timestamped RainViewer past-radar loop. Those real pixels are a wall-clock display layer only. The simulated rain desk integrates 1-, 3-, 6-, and 24-hour or storm-lifetime totals from fixed 15-minute model steps; it is not an observed precipitation analysis. Keys 1–9 switch layers.

    Category and impact

    The primary chip uses North Indian Ocean terminology from RSMC New Delhi's three-minute scale. Because the simulator remains calibrated to USA/JTWC one-minute sustained wind, its regional band carries an asterisk and is indicative only; no averaging-period or gust conversion is applied. Track colour remains a secondary Saffir–Simpson comparison palette. "Potential" marks the model's DeMaria–Kaplan maximum potential intensity for the water under the storm — a ceiling, not a forecast. City winds come from the parametric wind profile at each city's range and bearing; flash-flood risk is a threshold proxy on parametric storm-total rain, not a discharge or inundation model.
